## Conditional power, not an empirical promise

Reuse PR391's original integrated block SDs, multiplied by1.14014, the same
df127 normal-model upper-SD multiplier recorded in PR388. No new variance fit.
Choose counts in128-block increments using
`n = ((z(1-alpha) + z(1-0.2/15)) * SD_upper / distance_to_gate)^2`.
The design scenario is+5BB/100 on each primary and zero loss on each safeguard;
each test has at least98.67% nominal power, giving at least80% joint power by
the union bound under that model. Tests need not be independent for that bound.

For a primary, the conditional pass chance is98.7% at+5,34.9% at+2 and1.67% at0.
For check-call/pressure, it is98.7% at0 and0.42% at the−5 boundary; at−10 it is
negligible under this approximation. If the original−14.81 adverse effect
repeats, expect failure, not an80% chance to pass. No credible unconditional
pass probability is available from one focal lineage and sparse tails.

These are normal approximations for planning. The variance multiplier does not
bound unseen poker tails or different focal-seed variance, and nominal Student-t
coverage can be imperfect for heavy-tailed sparse block returns. Full success
probability is not a product of individual advertised powers or an observed
prediction. Smaller effects or higher actual variance mean lower power at the
same fixed counts; an inconclusive result is valid within budget.

This joint-power design is stricter than the earlier primary-only80%-per-test
scenarios. For comparison, the new primary alone at80% per-test power would use
4,736 blocks per lineage and about1.81h of measured scaled play across all three
lineages and both passes, before loads/replay. That is a sensitivity calculation,
not a second recommended campaign: it omits the safeguards and does not deliver
80% joint power. Do not attribute the full proposal's larger cost to a failure
of the measured2.4× evaluation-efficiency gain.
